# Financial app UX patterns for Filing Footnote

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-23

**Scope:** Public product/help surfaces for navigation and research workflows. Filing Footnote is an offline, bilingual reader for official filings.

## Evidence labels

- **Documented** — stated in the product owner's public help or feature documentation.
- **Observed** — visible in a public page's rendered content during this review. A crawlable public web page is not evidence of every native-app state.
- **Suggested** — a Filing Footnote adaptation inferred from the evidence and current product constraints.

## Reference patterns

### Apple's native section index

**Documented:** UIKit's table-view section index is a navigational index beside a plain-style table. The data source supplies index titles and maps a tapped title to a section; localized collation supplies section and index titles and sorts items. Apple describes the pattern for sequential lists such as Contacts. ([`sectionIndexTitles(for:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uitableviewdatasource/sectionindextitles%28for%3A%29), [`UILocalizedIndexedCollation`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uilocalizedindexedcollation))

**Suggested:** This is a strong fit for the glossary's sequential term list, not necessarily the small company list. Offer a Korean 초성 index in Korean mode and A–Z in English mode; map index taps to the first populated section, omit empty index destinations, and keep ordinary scrolling/search available. The Korean index is a product-specific extension of Apple's localized-section model, not an Apple-provided Hangul index. Verify accessibility and touch targets in the native implementation.

## Candidate adaptations, ordered for the current app

1. **Bilingual glossary jump index — high value, low scope.** Add an index rail to the longer glossary: 초성 groups in Korean and A–Z in English. Preserve one consistent term list and update headers/index labels with the language switch. Search remains useful for exact terms; the index supports browsing. Indexing and sectioning are suggested adaptations, not documented support for 초성 in Apple's API.
2. **Korea / US segmented control — high value.** Keep the company list as one screen, with a visible market segment and the current language switch separate. The segment filters the bundled company set; it does not imply live market coverage. This applies the market-context selector familiar from screeners to a compact fixed universe.
3. **A small, explicit company sort menu — high value.** Offer **Newest filing**, **Company name**, and **Market cap rank (as of [data date])**. Include notices in recency so it reflects the latest available company activity. Label rank with its effective date and suppress it when the bundle lacks a defensible dated value; it must never look live. Apple's sortable watchlists and the documented ranking/table controls in Stock Analysis and TradingView support the control pattern, while the exact choices are app-specific.
4. **Company-name-led news and event rows — high value.** Lead with the company name, then the filing/event title; show the date and source/type as supporting metadata. Keep the filing title more prominent than ticker/market decoration. This borrows company-first event navigation from Quartr and familiar-company search/discovery from Toss while retaining official filing content as the authority. For the present scope, “news” should mean the app's included official filings/notices, not a new third-party feed.
5. **Quarterly drill-down with period matching — high value, scoped to existing reports.** Start at the latest quarter, show the same quarter from the prior year beside it, and make the period explicit at the point of comparison. Keep the same filing attached to the values throughout the flow. Give annual figures an explicit entry point. Stock Analysis’s period selector illustrates how a visible period mode helps readers interpret each value.
6. **Consistent DART / SEC original-source controls — high trust value.** Put a source label and “Open original” action in the same position on every filing detail; identify DART versus SEC EDGAR and show that opening the source requires internet. Preserve corrected-version status and distinguish a filing's submission date from its reporting period. Consistency is in placement and interaction, not in flattening provider names, filing forms or correction rules.

## Further basics to evaluate

1. **Company and glossary search.** Match displayed Korean and English names and ticker aliases in one field. Keep rows name-led. Test that “Samsung,” “삼성,” and “005930” reach the same company, and that “EPS” finds its glossary entry. [Apple Stocks search](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iph1ac0b1bc/ios)
2. **Filter filings by type.** Use the existing filing categories for a short picker on News and the company page. Make the active filter visible and show a reset action for an empty result. Test finding dividends without scrolling past periodic reports. [DART search](https://dart.fss.or.kr/guide/contents122.jsp), [EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/)
3. **Resume the reading position.** After opening a filing and returning, restore the selected market, sort and scroll position. After a language switch, keep the same company or filing and translate its visible labels. Test returning from a filing near the bottom of News with VoiceOver focus on the originating row.
4. **Make filing versions easy to find.** A corrected filing should expose the previous version and distinguish the filed date from the period covered. Test getting to the original and amended documents from the correction detail. [DART search](https://dart.fss.or.kr/guide/contents122.jsp), [EDGAR search help](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/how-do-i-use-edgar)

## Design guardrails for adaptation

- Keep the existing product flow: **company → recent filings → filing summary and figures → figure detail/comparison → original source**. Research products are references for individual mechanisms, not replacement flows.
- Maintain the current visual identity and native, restrained list/detail hierarchy. The visual references are intentionally distinct: Stocks' native watchlist, Stock Analysis' dense ranking table, TradingView's configurable workspace, Quartr's event chronology, and DART/EDGAR's official search forms solve different jobs. Do not blend them into one dashboard.
- Keep bilingual labels paired where a term may be unfamiliar; preserve original report titles alongside plain-language categories. Company names and filing titles should survive the English/Korean switch without changing source identity.
- Keep all bundled data's “as of” date visible where recency or market-cap rank is implied. A local bundle is not a live market feed.
- Do not import accounts, trading, paid research, price charts, technical indicators, alerts, personalized ranking, or AI features from these references.

## Source limitations

The reviewed evidence is limited to public help/product pages, DART/SEC public search surfaces, and a provider-published Toss Securities video/feed. It does not include logged-in states, native-app hands-on interaction, usability studies, or verification of feature availability by account, locale or subscription. Toss's video is provider-authored and dated; it supports a design pattern, not current app parity. Filing Footnote’s dated market-cap ranking and newest-activity sort apply to its bundled company set.
